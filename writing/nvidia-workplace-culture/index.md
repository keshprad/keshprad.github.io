# What made “your life’s work” feel true at NVIDIA

Keshav Pradeep · November 22, 2026

NVIDIA is the place where you can do your life’s work. You’ve heard Jensen and other leaders at NVIDIA repeat this like it’s gospel. But any company’s leadership can make those types of claims, so what about NVIDIA made that promise feel *true to me*?

I spent ~15 months at NVIDIA before leaving earlier this year for grad school. It’s the only full-time job I’ve held, so these factors shape my perspective in this reflection.

While many have advocated for Jensen’s return, unfortunately, that may not happen. With that possibility in mind, I want to reflect on what I cherished about working at NVIDIA, and what I hope the company preserves if they ultimately progress with a new leader at the helm.

**Information flow:** NVIDIA operates in an “egg-like” transparency model: from the outside you can’t see anything; from the inside you can see most everything. I like the way [Nader described it in his post](https://x.com/NaderLikeLadder/status/2122063504647856159): you are *expected* to force information outside your org; people send weekly “top 5” emails to everyone at the company who might find their work useful or interesting, even if that ends up being thousands of people. Information flows through the company in an almost unpredictable manner much like a neural network. In this way, NVIDIA is a playground for engineers. That openness encouraged and cultivated my curiosity, even when it went beyond my own team’s work.

**Trust and autonomy:** Mission is the boss. Another core value to NVIDIA’s culture. I never felt I was doing something simply because my boss told me to. Nor did I feel I had to go through levels of bureaucracy to justify working on something. Instead, I was trusted to work on things which fit our mission, and expected to challenge projects if I felt they didn’t. At NVIDIA, we worked to accomplish our mission; all else was beside the point.

**Leadership Engagement:** Jensen’s engagement with employees’ work is perhaps the most unique characteristic of his leadership style, and one which makes him feel irreplaceable. At the same time, I believe this is the most important characteristic of NVIDIA to preserve. I repeatedly saw Jensen join email threads with a level of technical detail that no CEO of a 40,000+ employee company could be expected to keep up with. You don’t need to be a director or work on revenue-critical projects to get Jensen’s time. [Pavlo describes a similar experience](https://x.com/PavloMolchanov/status/2122368165376369047): Jensen replied even to interns’ emails, and people at any level could present to him directly. That kind of commitment and engagement from Jensen and other NVIDIA leaders made me realize the importance of my work. It felt like Jensen was present for all of us, whoever needed him that particular day.

**Looking forward:** This sense of purpose didn’t come from Jensen alone. The workplace culture he helped build has been embraced up and down the leadership chain. This gives me hope that the culture will endure. But with the potential of a new CEO on the horizon, preserving this culture will take a deliberate effort. Sharing information across orgs, trusting employees to pursue the mission, and leadership engagement with employees’ work -- these are all encoded into NVIDIA’s DNA. These are the reasons “your life’s work” felt true to me. Someday after grad school, if I return to NVIDIA this is the culture I hope to find once again.
